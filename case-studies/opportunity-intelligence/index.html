01 · PROBLEM FRAMING

Job search is a series of decisions spread across disconnected tools.

Finding a posting is only the entry point. A job seeker still has to decide whether the role fits, whether its scope is worth the effort, what evidence to tailor, whether a contact is relevant, and what to do next.

Observed task fragmentation

Job-board workflows, employer application forms, resumes, contact context, and follow-up reminders can live in separate places. LinkedIn’s tracker supports a pipeline and network visibility, while company-site applications require manual status updates. Public job-seeker discussions also describe spreadsheet upkeep and follow-up details getting lost; these are qualitative anecdotes, not prevalence measures.

Evidence boundary: I found enough evidence to treat tracking friction as real, but not enough to claim a universal pain level or a product-market gap.

Decision bottleneck

Role labels don’t reliably describe the work. In the Singapore examples reviewed, an “AI Transformation” Business Analyst role centered on programme governance, budget tracking, and reporting, while a customer-transformation BA role emphasized CRM delivery, requirements, UAT, and adoption.

  • Fit depends on responsibilities and eligibility, not only title keywords.
  • Career value needs role-level evidence and user judgment.
  • A relevant contact does not imply permission or readiness to refer.
02 · PRODUCT DEFINITION

Help the user allocate attention—not just record applications.

Primary user: a serious professional job seeker managing several plausible roles at once, with limited time and a need to balance fit, career direction, effort, and relationships. The demo profile is fictional and editable.

Core job to be done: “When several opportunities compete for my attention, help me understand which are worth pursuing, what evidence supports that view, and what I should do next—while keeping the final decision mine.”

Product hypothesis

A reviewable opportunity queue can help job seekers make better, more explainable decisions when it connects role-fit evidence, role-scope value, relationship context, resume strategy, and next action.

Boundaries

  • Not a job board or generic application tracker.
  • Not an auto-apply tool or message-sending CRM.
  • Priority is not a hiring probability.
  • Company and career-value signals are estimates, not reputation verdicts.
  • Private LinkedIn data is neither required nor included.
03 · PRIMARY USER JOURNEY

One opportunity, from inbox to next action.

The demo is organized around the decision sequence: inspect why a role matters, review relationship options, select a resume approach, then record a human decision.

01 · TriageChoose the next roleSort active opportunities by editable fit, career-value, and timing weights. See overdue work without letting urgency silently rewrite job quality.
02 · EvaluateReview the evidenceCompare strengths, gaps, confidence, role-scope signals, and the score breakdown.
03 · ConnectChoose a relationship actionReview contact relevance and history. Ask for insight, reconnect, connect, contact a recruiter, ask for a referral, or skip.
04 · PrepareChoose a resume strategySelect a variant and use specific tailoring prompts, without inventing experience.
05 · ProgressRecord what happenedLog outreach, complete or snooze work, override priority, add notes, and update the application stage.
04 · PRODUCT DECISIONS

Evidence → inference → decision.

These choices reduce duplication and keep uncertain judgments visible.

01

Evidence
LinkedIn, Huntr, and Teal already document job pipelines, notes, match support, job-linked materials, and contact context. Inference
A feature-only tracker proposition would be weakly differentiated. Decision
Make the center of the experience a justified attention decision connected to a human-owned next step.

02

Evidence
A relationship can be relevant without being recent, strong, or referral-ready. The demo’s contact signals are synthetic and incomplete. Inference
Network access should not make a job appear intrinsically better. Decision
Keep network context outside the priority formula; use it only to suggest a proportionate action.

03

Evidence
Job descriptions provide uneven evidence, and a match score cannot establish hiring outcomes. Inference
Users need to see why an assessment exists and what remains uncertain. Decision
Show strengths, gaps, confidence, and source context; label scores as triage aids, not probabilities.

04

Evidence
External actions such as applications and messages affect the user’s real prospects. Inference
Local simulation should not imply that outreach or submission occurred. Decision
Require the user to log what they sent and confirm an application stage; send nothing from the demo.

05 · AI + WORKFLOW BOUNDARIES

Use rules for state, AI for meaning, people for consequential judgment.

The public demo simulates assessment output with authored fixtures and local keyword matching; it does not call an AI model.

06 · FRONTEND ARCHITECTURE

A stable command surface over a replaceable intelligence layer.

The demo uses semantic HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript. A shared command component routes supported instructions through local adapters; a production orchestrator could select a model through an organization-governed, provider-neutral LLM gateway. JSON fixtures and browser-local state keep the demo self-contained.

User surface

Opportunity workspace

Inbox, opportunity brief, people, resume strategy, activity, and edit controls.

Data adapter

Local assessment + state

Loads sample JSON, computes deterministic priority, runs mock keyword assessment, and persists local edits.

Replaceable source

Mock API seam

JSON jobs, people, relationship links, resumes, and application stages. A production API can replace the fixture without changing the screen model.

No login, server-side data, live LinkedIn connection, message history, or external application information is used by this public build.

07 · VALIDATION

Test decisions, not just visual polish.

Prototype acceptance checks are distinct from user research. The demo is designed so the following tasks can be tested against a spreadsheet baseline using the same scenario and synthetic postings.

  • ✓Identify the highest-ranked role and explain its score using the visible weights.
  • ✓Find fit strengths and open questions, and distinguish confidence from hiring odds.
  • ✓Find the most relevant contact and explain why the suggested action is proportionate.
  • ✓Override a relationship action, resume variant, career-value view, or priority.
  • ✓Complete the workflow through outreach logging, preparation, and user-confirmed application stage.
  • ✓Find neglected opportunities, snooze a due action, and see local activity history.

Usability study criteria

In moderated sessions, measure task completion; time to identify a priority opportunity; whether users understand the ranking and uncertainty; whether they can choose and override a network action; whether the resume guidance changes their next step; and whether they prefer the workflow to their current spreadsheet or tracker. Compare the same task in Focusline and a basic tracker. No external user study or outcome claim is made by this portfolio demo.

Current limitation: prototype-level acceptance checks do not demonstrate improved application outcomes, user preference, or reduced search time. Those require testing with job seekers using real, redacted materials and a baseline workflow.
